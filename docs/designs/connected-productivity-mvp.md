## Problem Statement

The intended problem is fragmentation across tasks, notes, calendar planning, reminders, and recurring commitments. These items are related in everyday planning, but the user currently manages them across different tools. The user has not yet documented a typical day in enough detail to establish which tools are involved, how often information is re-entered, how much time is lost, or whether commitments have been missed. The problem and the benefit of an integrated workflow remain hypotheses to test.

## Demand Evidence

There is no demonstrated external demand yet. The user is still building the core features, does not use the app consistently, has not tested it with other users, and has not measured app switching or identified a specific missed commitment caused by the current workflow. The product vision and personal motivation are clear, but they are not evidence of willingness to adopt or pay.

## Status Quo

The user reports relying on different tools depending on whether they are managing tasks, checking a calendar, capturing notes, or setting reminders. The current sequence is not standardized, and the exact tools, handoffs, duplicate entry, time cost, and recurrence workarounds are undocumented. No specific existing apps should be treated as confirmed parts of the user's workflow; Google Clock, Google Keep, TickTick, and Microsoft Planner were named as product inspiration.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

The first intended user is the builder: a data scientist who also develops online courses and personal software projects and balances family commitments.

The narrowest workflow to test is planning one real commitment from capture through completion: keep its task visible in My Day, give it a date or time in Calendar, schedule a reminder where needed, and attach the supporting note. The hypothesis is that these linked pieces can be managed without re-entering the same commitment in separate places. This is a product direction, not validated demand or a claim about commercial fit.

## Constraints

- The app is in active MVP development; consistent personal use and outside-user testing have not started.
- The user chose a broad MVP direction, while also agreeing that a connected workflow must give the product a clear reason to exist.
- My Day should be the daily planning entry point, with Calendar, reminders, notes, and budget planning connected where useful.
- Keep the existing Flutter architecture, reminder and recurrence semantics, storage, notification scheduling, and note relationships as the source of truth. Do not create parallel models or scheduling systems merely to make the UI appear unified.
- Google Clock is a reference for clear time information and direct controls only. Keep Total Reminders' own identity; do not copy Google branding, assets, or exact layouts.
- Google sign-in, automatic light/dark behavior, notifications, and monetization are product goals mentioned by the user; their MVP scope and implementation status need confirmation during engineering planning.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: My Day-Led MVP on Existing Feature Architecture

Make My Day the main planning surface and connect it to the existing reminder and note capabilities with the smallest set of links. This is the minimal viable approach: it limits migration risk and can validate a daily workflow quickly. It was not selected because the user preferred deeper links across product areas rather than a lighter My Day-only integration.

### Approach B: Unified Planning Architecture

Design a shared planning workflow that links tasks, calendar dates, reminders, notes, and budget-related commitments while preserving feature-specific models and services where they already work. My Day remains the daily entry point. Budget stays in the broad MVP scope, including monthly budget tracking, expense planning, and financial reminders, but follows the first end-to-end connected workflow slice. This is the selected approach because it best matches the user's goal of managing related commitments in one place. It carries greater architectural and delivery risk; “unified” must mean consistent links and navigation, not a premature rewrite or a single oversized model.

### Approach C: Capture-First Command Center

Make fast capture the center of the MVP, then let users route each captured item to My Day, Calendar, Notes, or reminders. This could reduce friction if capture is the dominant real-world problem, but that has not been observed. It was not selected because the user chose a connected planning architecture and wants a clear daily planning experience.

## Recommended Approach

Proceed with the user-selected Approach B, anchored by My Day. The first end-to-end connected workflow slice is a task visible in My Day, its scheduled date/time reflected in Calendar, its reminder using the existing scheduling and recurrence behavior, and its supporting note remaining linked. Budget remains part of the broad MVP, with monthly budget tracking, expense planning, and financial reminders, but is delivered as a later feature slice rather than folded into this first flow. Build incrementally on existing services and persistence; settle model boundaries from code and tests before introducing any shared abstraction.

The approved sketch is a rough My Day hierarchy: current time and date, the next commitment with its linked note, today's checklist and completion state, then navigation to Calendar, Notes, and Budget. “Good enough” approves this direction for the design record, not a final visual design or a claim that the UI has been implemented.

## Open Questions

- What is the smallest shared planning concept that links existing data without conflating a task, a reminder, and a calendar occurrence?
- Which links must be editable from every surface, and which should navigate to the existing feature flow?
- What data model and existing feature surfaces should support the in-scope Budget Planner slice without duplicating reminder or calendar behavior?
- Which of Google sign-in, sync, notifications, and automatic theme behavior are required for the first usable release, and which can follow?
- What evidence from actual use would justify keeping My Day as the anchor or changing the product direction?
- No implementation estimate or distribution pipeline has been established. The earlier 3–6 month discussion was a rough team-scale illustration, not a scoped delivery estimate.

## Success Criteria

### Product behavior

- A real commitment can be planned once and accessed from My Day and its relevant Calendar date without creating a disconnected duplicate.
- A scheduled reminder continues to use the existing recurrence, persistence, notification, and alarm behavior.
- A supporting note can be opened from the commitment and remains linked after the app is restarted.
- The broad MVP includes monthly budget tracking, expense planning, and financial reminders; these are delivered after the first connected workflow slice and have explicit persistence and reminder coverage.
- The flow has automated coverage for creation, editing, navigation between linked surfaces, persistence, and reminder scheduling; existing reminder and recurrence tests remain green.

### Validation

- Complete a five-weekday workflow log using real work or personal commitments.
- For each observed commitment, record the tools used, each switch or duplicate entry, time spent, any late or missed step, and any recurrence workaround. Mark unobserved details as unknown.
- At the end, use the log to decide whether a repeated workflow problem was observed. Do not treat feature completion or the builder's preference as proof of external demand.
